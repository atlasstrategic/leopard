# Development status

## Playable now

Milestone-B local Three.js twin-engine handling, wind/handling tuning and scenario current, three cameras, Raymarine-inspired instruments and a fictional solid berth. Milestone-C batches: bounded contact/telemetry recorder and JSON/CSV exports; independent delayed fender crew with local cushioning; B01/B02 bow/stern mooring with tension-only fairlead forces, load warnings and failure; live approach → securing → secured states. Together with the batches below, these now form the **complete fuel mission loop** (holding → clearance → approach → secured → service → departure → debrief, with restart checkpoints), tracked in issue #1.

**Alongside correction:** after the first successful line attachment, the mint arrival target switches to a configurable amber alongside whole-hull envelope. The old 1.2 m approach-centre requirement no longer governs securing. Both lines need limited slack, safe load and idle crew; gentle correctly covered east-quay fender contact can count (hard/bare/wrong-obstacle contacts cannot). The active target stays alongside after both lines are released; Retry resets it. Take in/Ease adjusts paid-out length in bounded 0.25 m tasks at 0.12 m/s with neutral/speed/load/length limits, Stop, feedback and events. The vessel is never snapped toward a target.

**Show me:** separate calm-water default-boat run with explanatory steps for fenders, momentum/braking, arrival hold, first-line target switch, paired gradual tending and settling. It uses ordinary session physics, crew commands and objectives; no pose injection. Pause/Repeat/Take over/Return to practice; the original attempt remains preserved, paused. This shows one suitable bow-first method for these conditions, not universal seamanship.

**Harbour traffic (issue #1, step 1):** a scripted monohull starts at the fuel berth and, when released by the holding stage, backs off the quay and leaving to the south-west. It stops for the player's boat instead of pushing through. The contact solver now supports moving capsule obstacles using relative velocity; fixed-box contacts are numerically unchanged. Show me runs without traffic.

**Holding and clearance (issue #1, step 2):** attempts now start in a holding phase with harbour-radio announcements. A 5 s countdown runs while the boat's centre is inside the holding area south-east of the quay (resetting if it leaves); the monohull then departs, and the berth is called clear once the monohull has left the fuel berth and approach lane, which starts the existing approach. Early entry into the fuel zone costs +10 s per entry. Contact with the monohull on an uncovered hull point fails the mission (simulation stops until Retry); fender-covered contact is a normal penalty. Export schema version 2, scenario version 5.

**Fuel service (issue #1, step 3):** once first secured, a checklist runs engines off → diesel → fuel (180 L, accelerated) → pay → engines on. Out-of-order steps and petrol are refused with explanations. Engines off means no thrust and counts as neutral for securing. Losing secured stops fuelling (litres kept) and blocks further steps until re-secured; engines may be restarted at any time for safety. The Session option `traffic` is now `mission`, covering traffic, holding and service. Development builds expose `window.__leopard.lab` for scripted browser checks.

**Departure (issue #1, step 4):** the south training barrier is now a breakwater with an 18 m entrance marked by red (west) and green (east) lights, with training barriers enclosing the water outside. Completing the service starts the departure: let go both lines (no longer unsecures), clear the quay, and exit between the lights; crossing the gate in the port half of the channel costs +5 s. Crossing the gate completes the mission and stops the simulation. The monohull now exits through the entrance on its starboard side. Scenario version 6.

**Debrief and score (issue #1, step 5):** the session records debrief metrics (closest monohull gap, peak speed near the berth, countdown resets, fenders on arrival, lever changes, refused commands, lines let go under load). When the mission completes or fails, a debrief dialog shows a 0–100 score using the plan's 40/25/20/15 weighting, per-category deductions, key figures, penalty reasons, one tip from the largest weighted loss, and an overhead SVG trace of both tracks with contact points. Failed missions are not scored. Weights and thresholds are provisional (`scoreConfig`).

**Restart checkpoints (issue #1, step 6):** Approach (berth called clear) and Departure (service complete) checkpoints are saved automatically. Restart from either via buttons in the objective panel and debrief: the exact saved moment is restored as a new attempt continuing the earlier log; Retry still restarts the whole mission. The debrief counts restarts (not scored). This completes milestone C's mission loop.

**Stage plugins (issue #2), step 1 (stage file format):** the fuel mission's harbour layout, bollards, berth, zones, entrance gate, labels, conditions and monohull now live in `stages/fuel-dock/stage.json`, validated by a zod schema (`src/stage/schema.ts`) with cross-reference checks, and converted to engine units by `src/stage/load.ts`. `config.ts` derives its existing constants from the stage, so behaviour is unchanged (all values verified bit-identical). The gate crossing, entrance lights, labels and debrief map bounds now read the stage. `stages/stage.schema.json` is generated for editors; `npm run stage:validate` checks all stages. Adds the `zod` dependency (~28 kB gzipped). Also fixes the empty "Restart from" row showing before any checkpoint exists.

**Stage plugins (issue #2), step 2 (mission building blocks):** the fuel mission's objectives, radio calls, rules, checklist, scoring and checkpoints now live in the stage file as a list of steps (`holdInZone`, `waitForClear`, `arriveAtBerth`, `secureAlongside`, `checklist`, `exitThroughGate`) and rules (`keepOut`, `protectedContact`). The session runs whatever steps the stage lists; the coarse phase follows the step kind, so the docking logic is unchanged. The objective panel (`src/objective.ts`), checklist panel, checkpoint buttons, instrument bearing, scene zones/gate markings and debrief map and tips are all driven by the stage. `Session.skipTo(stepId)` replaces hand-edited test shortcuts. Show me runs the stage's berth steps only, with no radio. Checklist actions and events are renamed after item ids (e.g. `service.engines-off`, `service.fuel.done`). Stage version 7.0.0. The harbour entrance was also widened to 24 m (stage 6.1.0).

**Stage plugins (issue #2), step 3 (stage loading and picker):** the active stage is set once at startup (`useStage()` in `config.ts`, live bindings) from `?stage=<id>` or a validated local file (`?stage=file`, kept in session storage); a Stage panel offers the bundled stages (`src/stage/registry.ts`) and Load stage file…, with notices for unknown ids and invalid files. Import-time stage captures (checklist and checkpoint markup, mooring and debrief markup, traffic config) are now read lazily. Stages may have no traffic, zones or gates. The berth's quay face is derived from its alongside envelope and drives line attachment and quay decoration (previously the west face only; the fuel dock's north quay no longer shows stray fender posts). Show me is limited to the fuel dock, which it is scripted for.

**Stage plugins (issue #2), step 4 (authoring kit):** `STAGE_AUTHORING.md` (workflow, units, file structure, building blocks, worked example, checks, limits). `npm run stage:validate` now adds geometry checks (`src/stage/check.ts`): start pose clear, boat fits the approach target, alongside pose inside the envelope with starboard to the quay and both lines attachable, hold zones clear, reachability from the start for the boat's beam on a 1 m grid (berth, zones, both sides of each gate), and the traffic route simulated for clearance and completion; it also accepts file paths. `npm run stage:test` (`src/stage/playtest.ts`) plays each step with the real engine, moving the boat between steps, and names the step that fails. The playtest lets traffic leave before moving onto the berth or to the exit; it found that the fuel dock's clearance zone ends about 0.5 m from the approach target, which is harmless in play (the player comes from the holding area) but worth knowing.

**Stage plugins (issue #2), step 5 (independently authored stage):** an agent given only `STAGE_AUTHORING.md`, the schema and the example stages built **Open water · First session** (`stages/open-water/stage.json`): holds at marks A (ahead), B (astern) and C (turn), then a stop box; no traffic or mooring. Its findings: (1) an engine bug: a hold directly after another hold completed at once because the countdown carried over (fixed: each step starts with a fresh countdown; the playtest now fails holds that end early; regression test added); (2) `holdInZone` checks position only; (3) every stage must have a berth even without mooring; (4–7) guide gaps (last-step semantics, stand-alone approach target, controls and what the player sees, bearing default), now documented; also zone names were lowercased whole in texts ("mark a"), now only the first letter. Items 2–3 are left as format follow-ups.

**Boat models:** procedural models in `src/boats.ts` replace the capsules and boxes. The traffic monohull is a Beneteau Oceanis 38.1 (11.5 m, 3.99 m beam, plumb bow, wide transom, hull-window band and sheer stripe, low coachroof, twin wheels, teak cockpit, navy sprayhood, bimini and lazy bag, furled genoa, deck-stepped mast about 16.5 m above the waterline, guardrails, dinghy on the foredeck). It collides with exactly its visible deck-edge outline, generated from the same hull form as the model (`src/hulls.ts`); the contact solver, traffic clearance metric, keep-out zones and route check use the outline instead of a capsule, with a bounding-circle early exit so the solver stays cheap. The Leopard 42 has lofted plumb-bowed hulls within its contact footprint, hull windows and stripes, bridgedeck, saloon with a raked tinted band, hardtop over the cockpit, raised starboard helm (under the helm camera), trampoline and a rig about 20 m tall. The stage's monohull is now 11.5 × 3.99 m (stage version 7.2.0). The true outline, with its wide transom, clipped the quay as the monohull turned out of the berth (the capsule had hidden this), so its reversing waypoint moved from x 3.2 to 2.5; the route now clears every structure by more than 0.3 m, with no validator warning. The development handle also exposes the view for camera-positioned screenshots.

## Assumptions and next work

September 2024 Leopard 42 dimensional/equipment baseline, not a verified 2026 configuration. Planar low-speed dynamics, visual hull/rig, thrust/drag, line elasticity/failure, fitting capacity, crew rates and fender coverage are **provisional game parameters**, not calibrated or certified safety limits. Contact load is summed solver impulse per fixed tick divided by tick duration, **not physical peak force or damage**. Two lines are not full real-world mooring; no springs/breast lines, rope wrapping/chafe, simulated crew travel, waves, prop effects, depth or moving traffic. Browser feel and real hardware performance have not been validated by a skipper; no Windows/macOS/mobile acceptance.

Next: playtest and calibrate handling and the provisional score thresholds with experienced operators, then milestone D (a recognisable Croatian harbour and boat assets) and milestone E (browser hardening). No actual Croatian geography or real fuel-service claims yet. See `README.md` for controls, units and verification procedures.
